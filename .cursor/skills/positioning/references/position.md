# ch47 Position

Last updated: February 2026. Early stage.

## Who Jackson Is

Performance marketer turned builder. The career arc matters because it IS the positioning.

**The path:** Media buyer at 4AM (performance marketing agency) → agency work at Space Pilot/Little Pilot in Maine → co-founded Fungushead (SEO-driven mushroom spore e-commerce, peaked tens of thousands/month) → back at 4AM in analytical/systems role building custom Google Ads audit and optimization tools with Claude Code.

**The turn:** Mid-2025, started building with AI coding tools. Not as a developer. As a practitioner who needed things built. Websites, automation tools, email systems, a Google Ads agent. The instinct was always there (jailbreaking a PS3 at 12-13), but the tools finally caught up to the ambition.

**Credibility source:** Real projects with real revenue at stake. Fungushead was a business, not a side project. 4AM tools handle real ad spend. The Google Ads agent is being built to work in production, not to demo well. Everything Jackson knows about AI comes from using it to solve problems that existed before AI.

## The Thesis

The most dangerous people in AI aren't researchers or career engineers. They're domain experts who learn to build.

They know what the problems actually are because they've spent years inside them. Performance marketing, e-commerce, operations, whatever the domain. Now AI tools let them build their own solutions without a CS degree or a dev team. The gap between "I know what needs to exist" and "I can make it exist" just collapsed.

Most AI content ignores this. It's either engineers talking to engineers, or hype merchants selling transformation to people who won't build anything. The middle (practitioners who build, skeptics who ship) is underserved and growing fast.

Ch47 broadcasts from that middle.

**Corollary beliefs:**
- Agents are overhyped in demos, underhyped in practice. The real ones are boring and specific.
- Most AI content is noise. Recycled takes, engagement farming, "5 prompts" listicles. The bar is on the floor, which is an opportunity.
- The gap between demos and production is where all the interesting problems live, and almost nobody talks about it honestly.
- Building in public has real value, but only if the "public" part serves the "building," not the other way around.

## Who It's For

**Primary audience: Practitioner-builders.** People who use AI as a tool to build real things. Marketers, operators, small business owners, freelancers who've started coding with AI. They're skeptical of hype but optimistic about what's possible. They want to see the work, not the TED talk. They're in the arena, not the audience.

**Secondary audience: Curious skeptics.** People in adjacent roles (marketing, product, ops) who are AI-curious but haven't started building yet. They follow ch47 because the honesty and specificity builds trust. Some of them become practitioner-builders. Some just appreciate the perspective.

**Tribe identity:** Builders, tinkerers, skeptical optimists. People who use AI and are suspicious of anyone who talks about AI as a revolution. They self-select by recognizing the voice.

## Who It's Not For

- The "5 prompts to 10x your productivity" crowd.
- People who talk about AI without building with it.
- LinkedIn thought leaders performing expertise they don't practice.
- Pure engineers who gatekeep "real" development. (Jackson isn't pretending to be an engineer. The non-traditional path is the point.)
- Anyone who wants AI content that's comfortable, vague, or congratulatory.
- Corporate buyers looking for enterprise thought leadership.

## What ch47 Is

A personal broadcast. One person building with AI in public. Showing the process, the failures, the tools, the thinking. A frequency, not a company. Not a media brand. Not a VC-backed content play. One person with a microphone and a terminal.

**Format:** channel47.dev as hub. Substack weekly (Sunday). X 3-5x/week. LinkedIn 1-2x/week. Build logs over abstract commentary.

**Content pillars (ranked by priority):**
1. **Build logs.** What got built, what broke, what was learned. The primary differentiator. Screenshots, code, process. The receipts.
2. **Tool reports.** Tested, not reviewed. From use, not press releases.
3. **Contrarian takes.** Things the industry isn't saying, backed by experience.
4. **Process notes.** The thinking behind decisions. Why this, not that.

## What ch47 Is Not

- A tutorial channel. (Shows the work, doesn't teach step-by-step.)
- A news source. (Reacts to what matters, doesn't cover everything.)
- A growth hack account. (Quality earns attention. Tricks borrow it.)
- A company. (No team, no investors, no board. One person.)

## Competitive Landscape

**Direct space (AI builder-creators):** Crowded with hype merchants and prompt engineers. The serious builders (people shipping real tools) mostly don't create content. That's the gap. Ch47 sits where building and broadcasting overlap.

**Indirect space (tech newsletters/creators):** Stratechery (deep analysis, subscription model), Lenny's Newsletter (product management community), The Pragmatic Engineer (engineering depth). These are the quality bar, not the competition. They demonstrate that depth + voice + consistency = audience.

**The ch47 advantage:** Non-traditional path. Performance marketing background means the lens is ROI, not research. The projects are businesses, not demos. The voice is distinctive enough to be recognizable in two sentences. Most AI creators sound interchangeable. Ch47 shouldn't.

## Monetization (Open)

No locked thesis yet. That's correct for early stage. The options worth keeping in mind:

- **Consulting/freelance funnel.** Ch47 demonstrates the ability to build AI tools for marketing/business contexts. 4AM is already a version of this. Natural extension.
- **Products.** The Google Ads agent has a self-hosted paid model. Skills (like the ones Jackson builds) could become products. Templates, tools, workflows.
- **Community.** Vibe Marketers workshop is early signal. If practitioner-builders cluster around ch47, a community model could work.
- **Sponsorship/ads.** Only viable at scale. Not relevant now.

**Decision framework:** Don't pick a monetization model. Let 8-12 weeks of consistent publishing reveal what the audience actually wants. The right model will be obvious from inbound. Forcing it now optimizes for a thesis that hasn't been tested.

## Decision Filters

When evaluating whether something fits ch47, run it through these:

1. **The builder test.** Does this come from doing something, or from reading about something? First-hand only.
2. **The audience test.** Would a practitioner-builder care about this? Would it help them build something or think about building differently?
3. **The replaceability test.** Could any other AI newsletter have published this? If yes, it doesn't ship.
4. **The stage test.** Is this the right thing to focus on given the current stage (early, building audience, finding fit)? If it's a scale-stage activity, shelf it.
5. **The energy test.** Does this feel like building or performing? If performing, reconsider.
