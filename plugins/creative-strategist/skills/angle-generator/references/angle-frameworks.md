# Angle Frameworks — Deep Dive

## Pain-Agitation Angles

The most reliable category. Humans are more motivated to avoid pain than gain pleasure.

**How to find in research:**
- Most emotionally charged language in complaints
- Cascading consequences: "Dog barks -> neighbors complain -> I feel anxious -> I dread coming home"
- The deeper the cascade, the stronger the angle

**Sub-types:**
- **The Visible Pain**: Problems others can see (social embarrassment)
- **The Hidden Pain**: Problems only the customer knows about (private struggle)
- **The Compounding Pain**: Gets worse over time if unsolved
- **The Transferred Pain**: Affects someone the customer cares about

**Execution:** Agitate with empathy, not cruelty. Customer should feel "finally, someone understands" — not manipulated. Always resolve with hope.

## Failed-Solution Angles

Extremely effective in crowded markets. Taps into learned helplessness.

**How to find in research:**
- "I've tried X, Y, and Z and nothing works"
- The specific solutions they tried and WHY they failed
- The emotional toll of repeated failure

**Sub-types:**
- **The Exhausted Buyer**: How many things they've tried
- **The Money Pit**: How much they've wasted
- **The Time Thief**: How long they've been dealing with it
- **The Almost-Solution**: "X was close but fell short because..."

**Execution:** Name specific failed solutions — vague "other products" doesn't resonate. Explain why each failed, then why this one is different. Pairs well with Discovery angles.

## Trigger-Event Angles

Targets the moment someone transitions from passive awareness to active searching.

**How to find in research:**
- "I finally decided to buy when..."
- "The last straw was..."
- Life changes: new baby, new home, health scare

**Sub-types:**
- **The Breaking Point**: Problem becomes unbearable
- **The Social Trigger**: Someone else notices or comments
- **The Life Stage Trigger**: Life change makes it relevant
- **The Deadline Trigger**: Time pressure (event, season, visitors)

**Execution:** Start the ad at the trigger moment. Create instant recognition — viewer thinks "that happened to me." Urgency is built-in.

## Identity Angles

Targets how the problem makes the customer see themselves. Most durable motivator.

**How to find in research:**
- "I feel like a bad [parent/pet owner/person]"
- "People must think I'm [lazy/gross/incompetent]"
- "I want to be the kind of person who..."

**Sub-types:**
- **The Negative Identity**: "Stop being the person who..."
- **The Aspirational Identity**: "Become the person who..."
- **The Social Identity**: "What do others see?"
- **The Self-Perception Shift**: "You deserve to feel..."

**Execution:** Handle with care — emotionally potent. Product enables the identity shift, doesn't define them by the problem. Works best with transformation testimonials.

## Social Proof Angles

Leverages tendency to follow others. Effective for overcoming skepticism in low-trust categories.

**Sub-types:**
- **The Bandwagon**: "Everyone's switching to..."
- **The Authority**: "[Expert type] recommends..."
- **The Transformation**: Before and after stories
- **The Unexpected User**: "[Surprising group] discovered..."

## Discovery / Secret Angles

Positions product as a hidden solution. Taps into curiosity.

**Sub-types:**
- **The Hidden Method**: "The [industry] doesn't want you to know..."
- **The Accidental Discovery**: "A [scientist] stumbled on..."
- **The Ancient Wisdom**: "For centuries, [culture] has used..."
- **The Simple Fix**: "The solution was simpler than anyone thought"

**Execution:** The "secret" needs to be genuinely interesting, not manufactured. Works well in long-form formats (YouTube, articles).

## Comparison Angles

Direct or indirect comparison to alternatives.

**Sub-types:**
- **Direct**: Product vs. specific competitor
- **Category**: Product vs. the old way of doing things
- **Cost**: Product cost vs. cost of alternatives or not solving
- **Time**: Speed/ease vs. alternatives

## Specificity Angles

Concrete data points build instant credibility.

**Sub-types:**
- **The Stat**: Lead with a surprising statistic
- **The Timeframe**: "Works in 30 seconds"
- **The Count**: Number of customers, units sold
- **The Comparison Number**: "X times more effective"

**Execution:** Odd numbers feel more real than round numbers. Source the stat if possible. Combine with another angle type for maximum impact.
