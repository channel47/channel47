# Angle Frameworks — Deep Dive with Combinations and Execution

## Pain-Agitation Angles

The most reliable category. Humans are more motivated to avoid pain than gain pleasure.

**How to find in research:**
- Most emotionally charged language in complaints (🔥3 quotes)
- Cascading consequences: "Dog barks → neighbors complain → I feel anxious → I dread coming home"
- The deeper the cascade, the stronger the angle

**Sub-types:**
- **The Visible Pain**: Problems others can see (social embarrassment)
- **The Hidden Pain**: Problems only the customer knows about (private struggle)
- **The Compounding Pain**: Gets worse over time if unsolved
- **The Transferred Pain**: Affects someone the customer cares about

**Execution:** Agitate with empathy, not cruelty. Customer should feel "finally, someone understands" — not manipulated. Always resolve with hope. The hook presents the pain. The body agitates. The product resolves.

**Hook copy pattern:** "[Relatable pain statement in customer language]. [Agitation of consequence]. [Pivot to hope]."

**Failure mode:** If agitation exceeds empathy, the ad feels manipulative. If the pain is exaggerated beyond what the audience actually feels, credibility is lost. Test: would the persona nod in recognition, or wince at being stereotyped?

## Failed-Solution Angles

Extremely effective in crowded markets. Taps into learned helplessness.

**How to find in research:**
- "I've tried X, Y, and Z and nothing works"
- The specific solutions they tried and WHY they failed
- The emotional toll of repeated failure
- Look for personas with extensive "What They've Already Tried" sections

**Sub-types:**
- **The Exhausted Buyer**: How many things they've tried
- **The Money Pit**: How much they've wasted
- **The Time Thief**: How long they've been dealing with it
- **The Almost-Solution**: "X was close but fell short because..."

**Execution:** Name specific failed solutions — vague "other products" doesn't resonate. Explain WHY each failed (not just that it did), then why this product addresses the specific failure mode. Pairs naturally with Discovery angles.

**Hook copy pattern:** "[Name the failed solutions]. [Acknowledge the exhaustion]. [Introduce the difference]."
- Example: "Tried the sprays. Tried the scrub brush. Tried the 'professional grade' tablets. Still staring at the same ring. Here's why none of those worked — and what actually does."

**Failure mode:** Naming competitors directly can invite legal issues or position them as legitimate alternatives. Safer to name categories ("the sprays, the brushes") than brands unless the brand comparison is overwhelmingly positive.

## Trigger-Event Angles

Target the moment someone transitions from passive awareness to active searching.

**How to find in research:**
- "I finally decided to buy when..."
- "The last straw was..."
- Life changes: new baby, new home, health scare
- Look for 🔥3 quotes tagged with journey stage shifts (Pre-aware → Problem-aware, or Problem-aware → Solution-aware)

**Sub-types:**
- **The Breaking Point**: Problem becomes unbearable
- **The Social Trigger**: Someone else notices or comments
- **The Life Stage Trigger**: Life change makes it relevant
- **The Deadline Trigger**: Time pressure (event, season, visitors)

**Execution:** Start the ad AT the trigger moment. Create instant recognition — viewer thinks "that happened to me." Urgency is built-in because the trigger is happening NOW.

**Hook copy pattern:** "[The trigger moment, described vividly]. [Recognition beat]. [Transition to solution]."
- Example: "Your neighbor just left a note on your door about the barking. Again."
- Example: "Guests are coming this weekend and you just noticed the toilet ring."

**Failure mode:** If the trigger is too niche, reach is limited. If it's too generic, the recognition moment is lost. The best triggers are specific enough to feel personal but common enough to reach volume.

## Identity Angles

Target how the problem makes the customer see themselves. Most durable motivator.

**How to find in research:**
- "I feel like a bad [parent/pet owner/person]"
- "People must think I'm [lazy/gross/incompetent]"
- "I want to be the kind of person who..."
- Language cluster analysis: look for self-referential language in frustration and hope clusters

**Sub-types:**
- **The Negative Identity**: "Stop being the person who..."
- **The Aspirational Identity**: "Become the person who..."
- **The Social Identity**: "What do others see when they look at you?"
- **The Self-Perception Shift**: "You deserve to feel..."

**Execution:** Handle with care — emotionally potent. Product enables the identity shift, doesn't define them by the problem. Works best with transformation testimonials. Never shame.

**Hook copy pattern:** "[Identity tension]. [What it feels like]. [The shift]."
- Example: "I was the person who couldn't even have friends over because of my dog. Not anymore."

**Failure mode:** Shame is close to identity but toxic. "Are you the kind of person who can't even keep a clean house?" is an attack, not an ad. Test: does this make the persona feel seen, or exposed?

## Social Proof Angles

Leverage tendency to follow others. Effective for overcoming skepticism in low-trust categories.

**How to find in research:**
- Look for personas whose conviction pattern requires peer validation
- Check for quotes where customers reference other customers' experiences
- Review count data, star distributions

**Sub-types:**
- **The Bandwagon**: "Everyone's switching to..."
- **The Authority**: "[Expert type] recommends..."
- **The Transformation**: Before and after stories
- **The Unexpected User**: "[Surprising group] discovered..."

**Hook copy pattern:** "[Social proof data point]. [Why it matters]. [Implied recommendation]."
- Example: "47,000 dog owners tried everything else first. Here's what finally worked."

**Failure mode:** Generic social proof ("thousands of happy customers!") is invisible. Specific and surprising social proof works ("my VET recommended this" from a persona that trusts authority).

## Discovery / Secret Angles

Position product as a hidden solution. Taps into curiosity.

**How to find in research:**
- Product has genuinely novel mechanism, ingredient, or approach
- Check Surprising Findings from research — anything counterintuitive?
- Customers describe being surprised by how it works

**Sub-types:**
- **The Hidden Method**: "The [industry] doesn't want you to know..."
- **The Accidental Discovery**: "A [scientist] stumbled on..."
- **The Ancient Wisdom**: "For centuries, [culture] has used..."
- **The Simple Fix**: "The solution was simpler than anyone thought"

**Execution:** The "secret" needs to be genuinely interesting, not manufactured. Works well in long-form formats (YouTube, articles, landing pages). Curiosity gap must deliver — if the reveal is disappointing, trust is destroyed.

**Failure mode:** Manufactured secrets feel like clickbait. "Doctors don't want you to know" is overused and damages credibility. The discovery must be real and verifiable.

## Comparison Angles

Direct or indirect comparison to alternatives.

**How to find in research:**
- Competitive positioning map from research synthesis
- Quotes where customers directly compare products
- Clear, demonstrable advantages in the data

**Sub-types:**
- **Direct**: Product vs. specific competitor
- **Category**: Product vs. the old way of doing things
- **Cost**: Product cost vs. cost of alternatives (or cost of NOT solving)
- **Time**: Speed/ease vs. alternatives

**Hook copy pattern:** "[Alternative approach and its flaw]. [This product's advantage]. [Proof]."
- Example: "You could scrub for 20 minutes with a brush. Or drop this in and walk away."

**Failure mode:** Direct competitor naming can backfire (free advertising for them, legal risk). Category comparison is usually safer and broader. Cost comparison must be honest — "cheaper" only works if total value equation is clear.

---

## Specificity Angles

Concrete data points build instant credibility.

**How to find in research:**
- Product has surprising stats, timeframes, or counts
- Research contains specific numbers customers cite
- Review data (star counts, review volumes)

**Sub-types:**
- **The Stat**: Lead with a surprising statistic
- **The Timeframe**: "Works in 30 seconds"
- **The Count**: Number of customers, units sold
- **The Comparison Number**: "X times more effective"

**Execution:** Odd numbers feel more real than round numbers (4,847 > "nearly 5,000"). Source the stat if possible. Specificity is most powerful when combined with another angle category.

**Failure mode:** Unverifiable claims destroy credibility. "3x more effective" needs a reference point. Avoid superlatives without evidence ("the best", "the most powerful").

## Angle Combination Patterns

The best-performing ads combine two angle categories. These combinations are not additive — they're multiplicative, because each category addresses a different psychological lever.

### Tier 1 Combinations (highest-performing)

| Combination | Mechanism | Hook Structure | Best For |
|-------------|-----------|---------------|----------|
| **Failed-Solution + Discovery** | Validate exhaustion → introduce the new | "Nothing worked... because they all [shared flaw]. This is different because [novel mechanism]." | Veteran buyers in crowded markets |
| **Trigger-Event + Pain-Agitation** | Start at the moment → deepen the stakes | "[Vivid trigger moment]. And here's the part nobody talks about: [cascading consequence]." | Emotionally charged purchase moments |
| **Identity + Social Proof** | Aspiration → validation | "I used to be the person who [negative identity]. [X thousand people] already made the switch." | Status-conscious buyers |
| **Pain-Agitation + Comparison** | Feel the pain → see the better way | "[Pain in their words]. The old way: [alternative and its flaws]. The new way: [product advantage]." | Category disruptors |

### Tier 2 Combinations (strong)

| Combination | Mechanism | Best For |
|-------------|-----------|----------|
| **Comparison + Specificity** | Concrete advantage with proof | Products with measurable superiority |
| **Trigger-Event + Identity** | "The moment I realized I didn't want to be this person anymore" | High-emotion, life-change categories |
| **Failed-Solution + Social Proof** | "We all tried the same things. Then 47K of us found this." | Community-driven categories |
| **Discovery + Specificity** | "The hidden method: works in 30 seconds" | Novel products with concrete claims |

### How to identify combinations in research
1. Find 🔥3 quotes — which two categories does each quote touch?
2. Read persona decision journey monologues — the arc often crosses two categories naturally
3. Check if the persona's "lead with" (from creative brief) maps to one category and "prove with" maps to another — that's a natural combination

## Platform-Specific Execution Notes

Platform doesn't just determine where an ad runs — it shapes how the angle must be executed.

### Meta (Feed — image or video)
- Hook is visual-first. The image or first frame must communicate the angle before any text is read.
- Copy is secondary to thumb-stop. Punchy first line, then story/proof.
- Carousel works for Failed-Solution (slide per failed solution → reveal) and Comparison (side-by-side).

### Meta (Stories / Reels)
- Vertical 9:16. First 2 seconds are the entire hook.
- Text overlays are primary — many watch without sound.
- UGC aesthetic outperforms polished for most DTC products.

### TikTok
- Must not look like an ad. Native format or death.
- Trend-jacking works for Discovery and Social Proof angles.
- Stitch/duet format works for Comparison and Failed-Solution.
- Hook must work without context — viewers don't know the product yet.

### YouTube (Pre-roll)
- 5 seconds before skip. The hook must earn the next 25 seconds.
- Trigger-Event and Pain-Agitation work best — instant recognition holds attention.
- Long enough for story arcs: Failed-Solution → Discovery → Social Proof sequences.

### Google Search
- Intent-aligned. The ad must match what the searcher already wants.
- Specificity angles dominate ("Works in 30 seconds" > "Best solution").
- Comparison angles work for competitor keywords.
- Headline: hook. Description: proof. CTA: conversion.

### Google Performance Max / Display
- Visual-heavy. Image must carry the angle independently.
- Pain-Agitation and Identity angles work as single-image concepts.
- Headline variations should test different angle categories.
