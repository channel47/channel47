# Persona Framework — Calibration Examples

These examples show the depth, specificity, and actionability expected from data-driven personas. Note how each ends with a creative brief that a copywriter could immediately act on.

---

## Example 1: Pet Training Device

### Persona: "The Burned Buyer"

**The Snapshot**
- Age Range: 30-45
- Gender Skew: ~70% female
- Life Situation: Works from home, dog is a rescue or COVID adoption. Apartment/townhouse with shared walls.
- Income Signal: Willing to spend $30-80 but needs proof first. Already wasted money on training that didn't work.
- Journey Entry Point: Solution-aware — has tried other products, actively comparing
- Prior Solution History: Veteran — 3+ failed attempts (YouTube videos, trainer treats, a $200 trainer, maybe another device)

**The Decision Journey Monologue**
> "I've had Biscuit for two years now and the barking has gotten worse, not better. My neighbor left a note on my door last week and I was mortified. I've already tried those YouTube trainer methods — they worked for maybe a day. I hired a trainer for $200 and he was good while she was here but went right back to barking the next week. I'm googling 'ultrasonic bark device reviews' at midnight because I can't take another complaint. But honestly? I don't believe any of these things work anymore. I've seen too many products promise 'instant results.' If I buy another thing that doesn't work I'm going to lose it. I need to see REAL people saying this worked for their dog — not paid reviews, actual dog owners who were in my exact situation."

**The Trigger Event**
- Primary: Neighbor complaint (note, awkward encounter, formal complaint) — 🔥3
- Secondary: Embarrassment during walks, unable to have guests over
- Trigger frequency: Recurring — each complaint compounds the urgency

**Pain Points (ranked by intensity)**
1. "I've tried everything and nothing works" — 🔥3 | Journey: Solution-aware
2. Social embarrassment — "my neighbors must hate me" — 🔥3 | Journey: Problem-aware
3. Feeling like a failure as a pet owner — 🔥2 | Journey: Post-purchase (of failed solutions)

**Desired Outcome**
- Stated: "I want my dog to stop barking"
- Deeper: "I want to enjoy having a dog again and stop feeling constant anxiety about bothering people"
- Evidence: "I just want to be able to relax in my own home without dreading the next bark" — Trustpilot

**Objections & Skepticism**
1. "These things never work — I've tried everything" — 🔥3 | Overcome with: before/after video testimonials from similar situations
2. "Is it going to hurt my dog?" — 🔥2 | Overcome with: vet endorsement or safety certification
3. "It probably only works for some dogs, not mine" — 🔥2 | Overcome with: variety of breeds in testimonials

**What They've Already Tried**
- YouTube training methods — failed because: requires consistency they can't maintain while working
- Professional trainer ($200+) — failed because: results didn't stick after sessions ended
- Citronella collar — failed because: dog got used to it
- Previous ultrasonic device (cheap Amazon one) — failed because: range too short, dog ignored it

**Competitive Relationship**
- Current alternative: Considering another ultrasonic device but deeply skeptical
- What they like: The concept of non-harmful bark reduction
- What they wish were different: "I wish I could try it and return it if it doesn't work for MY dog"
- Switching barrier: Trust — need overwhelming proof this one is different

**Language Fingerprint**
- Frustration: "I've tried everything", "at my breaking point", "nothing works", "waste of money"
- Hope: "game changer", "finally found something", "actually works"
- Skepticism: "does this actually work?", "is it just another gimmick?", "real reviews not fake ones"

**Attention & Platform Patterns**
- Where they search: Google ("best bark device reddit"), Reddit (r/dogs, r/dogtraining), Amazon reviews
- Content they trust: Long-form Reddit threads with replies, video reviews, real testimonials (not polished brand content)
- When they're receptive: Late night (post-barking-incident frustration), weekend mornings (research mode)
- Platform affinity: Meta (scroll-stopping video), YouTube (pre-roll during dog content)

**Creative Brief for This Persona**

| Field | Value |
|-------|-------|
| Lead with | "Tried everything?" — validate their exhaustion, acknowledge the failed-solution journey |
| Prove with | Video testimonials from real owners who also tried everything + money-back guarantee |
| Avoid | Polished brand aesthetics (reads as "too good to be true"), claims of "instant results" |
| CTA style | Risk-reversal — "30 days. Full refund if it doesn't work for YOUR dog." |
| Best platform | Meta (video ad showing real transformation) → retarget on Google (branded search) |
| Hook archetype | Empathy + failed-solution ("I was about to give up on my dog...") |

## Example 2: Toilet Cleaning Product

### Persona: "The Reluctant Cleaner"

**The Snapshot**
- Age Range: 28-40
- Gender Skew: ~55% male (research challenges the assumption this is a female-skewed category)
- Life Situation: Lives with partner. Recently took on bathroom cleaning duties.
- Income Signal: Not price-sensitive for cleaning products. Will pay more for less effort.
- Journey Entry Point: Problem-aware — knows they have a stain problem, doesn't know what products exist
- Prior Solution History: Naive — used whatever was under the sink, maybe one store-brand attempt

**The Decision Journey Monologue**
> "I didn't even know toilet rings were a thing until I actually started cleaning the bathroom. Sarah mentioned the toilet looked gross and I was like, fine, I'll clean it. Twenty minutes of scrubbing with that blue gel stuff and it still looks the same. I googled 'how to remove toilet ring' and apparently it's hard water? I don't even know what that means. Reddit says to use pumice stone but that sounds like it'll scratch everything. There's gotta be something I can just drop in and have it handle itself. I don't want to become a toilet expert, I just want it to not be embarrassing when people come over."

**The Trigger Event**
- Primary: Visible stain that won't come off with normal cleaning — 🔥2
- Secondary: Partner's comment, guests coming over
- Trigger frequency: Recurring — the stain comes back

**Pain Points (ranked by intensity)**
1. "Regular products don't actually clean tough stains" — 🔥2 | Journey: Problem-aware
2. Scrubbing is gross and feels pointless — 🔥2 | Journey: Problem-aware
3. Embarrassment when guests use the bathroom — 🔥3 | Journey: Pre-aware (didn't know until it happened)

**Desired Outcome**
- Stated: "I want a clean toilet without effort"
- Deeper: "I want to feel like a competent adult without spending my life cleaning"
- Evidence: "I just need something I can drop in and forget about" — Reddit

**Objections & Skepticism**
1. "Is this just another cleaning product that doesn't work?" — 🔥1 | Overcome with: video demo showing before/after
2. "Will it damage the toilet?" — 🔥1 | Overcome with: "safe for all toilets" + material compatibility claim

**What They've Already Tried**
- Store-brand toilet bowl cleaner — failed because: doesn't touch hard water stains
- Scrubbing harder — failed because: effort-to-result ratio is terrible

**Competitive Relationship**
- Current alternative: Generic toilet bowl gel cleaner
- What they like: It's cheap and available
- What they wish were different: "I wish it actually removed the stains instead of just making it smell clean"
- Switching barrier: Almost none — low brand loyalty in this category

**Language Fingerprint**
- Frustration: "nothing works on hard water stains", "I've been scrubbing for 20 minutes"
- Hope: "drop it in and forget about it", "just handles itself"
- Skepticism: "is this legit or another gimmick?"

**Attention & Platform Patterns**
- Where they search: Google ("how to remove toilet ring"), Reddit (r/CleaningTips), TikTok (cleaning hacks)
- Content they trust: Quick demo videos, before/after photos, Reddit recommendations
- When they're receptive: After a failed cleaning attempt, weekend mornings
- Platform affinity: TikTok (demo content), Meta (scroll-stopping before/after)

**Creative Brief for This Persona**

| Field | Value |
|-------|-------|
| Lead with | The visual — show the gross stain, show the effortless fix |
| Prove with | Real-time demo video (drop it in, time-lapse, stain gone) |
| Avoid | Making them feel bad about their cleaning habits — humor works, shame doesn't |
| CTA style | Low-friction curiosity — "Try it on your worst stain" |
| Best platform | TikTok (demo content that looks organic) → Meta (retarget with UGC) |
| Hook archetype | Curiosity + demonstration ("Watch what happens when you drop this in...") |

## Example Anti-Persona: Pet Training Device

### Anti-Persona: "The Positive-Only Purist"

**Who they are**
Dog owners deeply committed to positive-reinforcement-only training philosophy. Active in training communities, often have multiple dogs, follow specific trainers online.

**Why they'll never convert**
- Philosophically opposed to any device that deters behavior (even non-harmful). Will view ultrasonic devices as "punishment."
- No amount of safety data or vet endorsements changes a philosophical position.
- Will leave negative reviews and comments on ads, increasing CAC for other audiences.

**How to recognize them in targeting**
- Follow positive-reinforcement training accounts
- Use language like "force-free", "R+ only", "fear-free certified"
- Active in Facebook training groups with strong anti-device norms

**Research evidence**
- "I would NEVER use any kind of aversive on my dog. Period." — Reddit r/dogtraining
- "These devices work through fear. There's no such thing as a 'humane' bark deterrent." — forum post

## Calibration Checklist

When building personas, check against these examples for:

1. **Behavioral clustering** — personas differ on what they've tried and how they decide, not just age/gender
2. **Decision journey monologue** — traces trigger → search → evaluate → hesitate → decide, not just emotions
3. **Pain hierarchy** — ranked by intensity (🔥 score), not just frequency
4. **Stated vs. deeper desire** — always separated, with evidence quote
5. **Specific objections** — in their words, with what would overcome each
6. **Language fingerprint** — organized by emotional register (frustration/hope/skepticism), not a flat list
7. **Attention patterns** — where they search, what content they trust, when they're receptive
8. **Creative brief** — table format (Field | Value), specific enough that a copywriter could start writing immediately
9. **Anti-persona** — included with structural (not just preferential) reasons for exclusion
