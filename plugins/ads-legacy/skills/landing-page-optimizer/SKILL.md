# Landing Page Copy Optimizer

Your best copy already exists. It's in customer reviews, Reddit threads, support tickets—the exact words people use when frustrated, hopeful, or desperate for a solution.

Most landing pages fail because copywriters invent language instead of mining it. They write for everyone and connect with no one. They dump features, add corporate buzzwords, and produce copy that signals "AI generated this and nobody cared enough to make it real."

This skill teaches you to extract customer language, match awareness stages, eliminate friction, and write copy that converts. Not analysis documents. Not comprehensive reports. Copy.

---

## Why Default Landing Page Copy Fails

Without guidance, I produce:

- **Em dashes everywhere**: "This doesn't just improve X—it transforms Y"
- **AI buzzwords**: "Delve," "leverage," "robust," "seamless," "navigate," "unlock"
- **Corporate transitions**: "Furthermore," "Moreover," "Indeed"
- **Feature dumping**: Lists of what it does, not what it means
- **Vague claims**: "Many customers," "fast results," "better performance"
- **Uniform sentences**: Perfect grammar, no rhythm, no burstiness
- **Me-focused copy**: "We've worked with 500 companies. Our platform is industry-leading."

The result? Copy that destroys trust. And trust is conversion's oxygen.

## Core Principles

These aren't rules to follow—they're how expert copywriters think.

### 1. Mine Customer Language (Don't Invent It)

Joanna Wiebe spent 2-3 hours reading 500+ Amazon reviews for books about addiction recovery. One direct quote became the headline: "If you think you need rehab, you do." Result: **400% more clicks, 20% more conversions.**

**The Message Mining Method:**

Create a spreadsheet with columns:
- **Raw quote** — Exact customer language, word-for-word
- **Category** — Pain point, objection, desired outcome, transformation
- **Before state** — Problems, anxieties at the beginning
- **Dream state** — Desired outcomes, positive language
- **Swipeable** — Phrases ready for direct use

**Where to mine (ordered by authenticity):**
1. Amazon reviews for competitor/adjacent products
2. Reddit threads: "[category] recommendations"
3. G2/Capterra/Trustpilot reviews
4. YouTube comments on competitor videos
5. Support tickets and live chat logs

**What to extract:**
- Repeated phrases across multiple reviews (these are "sticky")
- Emotional language describing frustration or relief
- Specific outcomes with numbers and timeframes
- Objections they had before buying
- How they describe the product in their own words

**Why it works (neuroscience):** When prospects see their exact language reflected back, they recognize themselves. It bypasses the skeptical left brain and speaks directly to the emotional right brain where decisions actually happen.

### 2. Match Awareness Stage

You can't speak the same way to someone who doesn't know they have a problem as you do to someone ready to buy.

| Stage | What They Know | Lead With |
|-------|----------------|-----------|
| **Unaware** | Don't know they have a problem | Story, relatable scenario |
| **Problem-aware** | Know the problem, not solutions | The pain, what they're losing |
| **Solution-aware** | Researching options | Your unique mechanism |
| **Product-aware** | Know you, need convincing | Social proof, objection handling |
| **Most-aware** | Ready to buy | The offer, urgency, guarantee |

Most landing pages from ads target **solution-aware** or **product-aware** traffic. Lead with differentiation and proof, not education.

---

### 3. Pain > Pleasure (2:1 Ratio)

Kahneman and Tversky proved the psychological pain of losing something is **twice as intense** as the pleasure of gaining something equivalent. The amygdala—your brain's threat-processing center—responds to potential losses with the same circuitry it uses for fear.

**The hierarchy:**
1. **Present-tense pain** (most powerful): "You're wasting 30% of your time"
2. **Future pain avoidance** (strong): "Don't let this become a bigger problem"
3. **Future pleasure** (weakest): "You'll gain 30% more productivity"

Gary Halbert called this the "starving crowd principle": The biggest advantage isn't great copy—it's a desperate audience.

**Example:**
- Weak: "Gain clearer skin in 30 days"
- Strong: "Stop hiding your face in photos. Clear skin in 30 days."

---

### 4. Specificity = Credibility

Claude Hopkins (1866): "78-second shave" beats "fastest shave." A century later, nothing has changed.

**Why it works:** The brain processes concrete details as more verifiable. Vague claims trigger skepticism because they can't be confirmed. Specific numbers activate "proof-seeking" behavior—readers feel they can verify.

**Data:**
- Adding numbers to headlines increases clicks by 36%
- "Join 172,783+ professionals" increased sales 26.8% vs generic headline
- Reading level at 5th-7th grade converts at **11.1%** vs 5.3% for college level

**Never say:**
- "Many" → say "847"
- "Quick" → say "14 days"
- "Better" → say "3x faster"

---

### 5. Features + Benefits (Not One or the Other)

95% of purchasing decisions originate in emotional brain regions before rational justification occurs (Harvard neuroscience). People decide emotionally, then justify rationally.

- **Benefits** trigger the decision (emotional transformation)
- **Features** provide ammunition for justification

**The format:** Feature → Bridge → Benefit

"67.5 Million CFU proprietary blend (feature) works from your gut up (bridge), so you stop obsessing over whether people can smell your lunch (benefit)"

**The "So What?" Ladder:**
- Feature: 2-day shipping
- So what? → Products arrive quickly
- So what? → You get what you need without waiting
- Benefit: No more anxious tracking. Instant gratification.

### 6. Eliminate All Four Frictions (Peep Laja)

| Friction | Signal | Fix |
|----------|--------|-----|
| **Confusion** | "I don't understand what this does" | Clarify headline, add subheadline, simplify |
| **Uncertainty** | "Will this work for me?" | Use cases, testimonials matching their situation |
| **Anxiety** | "Can I trust this?" | Guarantees, trust badges, transparency |
| **Distraction** | "Something else caught my attention" | Remove unnecessary links, one CTA per section |

**Case study:** Moving the form above the fold—nothing else changed—produced a **309% increase** in conversions (Sierra Nevada University).

---

### 7. Kill AI Tells

70% of people say AI content makes it harder to trust what they see online. When copy feels AI-generated, trust evaporates.

**Words that scream AI (2025-2026):**
- "Delve" (207x more frequent in AI than human writing)
- "Robust," "seamless," "leverage," "unlock," "navigate"
- "In today's fast-paced world" (40%+ of AI intros)
- "Furthermore," "Moreover," "Indeed"
- "It's important to note that..."

**Structural tells:**
- Three-part lists everywhere
- Uniform sentence lengths (no burstiness)
- Perfect grammar with no personality
- "Emily" or "Sarah" as placeholder names (60-70% of AI articles)

**How to write like a human:**
- Use contractions ("it's" not "it is")
- Mix sentence lengths: Short. Medium flow. And occasionally longer sentences that build momentum toward a point.
- Ask questions ("Ever notice how...")
- Admit flaws ("This isn't for everyone")
- Include personal anecdotes or specific observations

**The test:** Read it out loud. Would you actually say this to a friend?

### 8. Social Proof: C-R-A-V-E-N-S

| Element | Bad Example | Good Example |
|---------|-------------|--------------|
| **C**redible | "John D." | "Sarah Martinez, Portland OR, Verified Purchase" |
| **R**elevant | Generic praise | "As a busy mom of 3..." (if targeting moms) |
| **A**ttractive (emotionally) | "Great product!" | "I was skeptical after 4 other probiotics failed..." |
| **V**isual | Text only | Photo, video, real face |
| **E**numerated | "Lost weight" | "Lost 14 pounds in 6 weeks" |
| **N**earby | Anonymous | "Fellow founder," specific location |
| **S**pecific | "Game-changer" | "Day 14: My girlfriend said I could kiss her in the morning again" |

**Placement:** Put social proof right before or after friction points. Price objection? → Testimonial about value. Effectiveness doubt? → Before/after results.

---

### 9. First-Person CTAs

Using "me" or "my" in CTAs creates psychological ownership before purchase. Personalized CTAs convert **202% better** than generic.

- Generic: "Start Your Free Trial" → "Start My Free Trial"
- Generic: "Get Your Free Guide" → "Get My Free Guide"
- Generic: "Submit" → "Yes, I Want Fresh Breath"

---

### 10. Structure Follows Attention

People scan, not read. If they can't understand value in one glance at the hero, they're gone.

**Proven flow:**
1. **Hero** — Benefit headline, subheadline (overcomes objection), visual, CTA. Value clear without scrolling.
2. **Value prop** — 3-5 key benefits as outcomes, not features
3. **Social proof** — Testimonials with photos, logos, specific results
4. **Conversion section** — CTA, form, trust signals (guarantee, secure checkout)
5. **Objection handling** — How it works, FAQ addressing real concerns
6. **Final CTA** — Restate offer with urgency, summarize transformation

## Format Types

Different formats need different treatment.

### Single-Product Landing Page
Classic direct response. One product, one offer, one CTA. Use all principles above directly.

### Listicle / Comparison Page
Hybrid: blog post + sales page. Reads like editorial content that educates while promoting.

**Key differences:**
- **Editorial tone** — "I tested 5 products" not "Buy our product"
- **Education first** — Build trust through information before asking
- **Comparison table** — Your product first, highlight differentiators
- **Lower CTR (1-8%) but higher conversion** — Readers are better qualified
- **Match traffic source** — TikTok traffic → short listicle; Google → longer form

**The trust transfer:** You're not just advertising—you're borrowing the credibility of editorial content. Break that voice and you break the trust.

### Advertorial
Native advertising disguised as content. Mirror the tone and style of the publisher's regular editorial.

**Key elements:**
- Personal story arc (struggle → discovery → transformation)
- Specific details that feel lived, not manufactured
- Scarcity/urgency woven naturally ("I tried to tell my friend but it was sold out")
- Transparency balance — feel authentic without being obviously promotional

## Anti-Patterns to Kill

| Pattern | Why It Fails | Fix |
|---------|--------------|-----|
| **"Unlock," "leverage," "delve," "robust"** | AI tells, signals low effort | Simple words: "get," "use," "explore," "strong" |
| **Em dash overuse** | "X—it does Y" is AI signature | Periods. Short sentences. |
| **Me-focused copy** | "I worked at X, I'm an expert" | You-focused: "You're struggling with X" |
| **Feature dumping** | Lists what, not why | So What? ladder for each |
| **Vague claims** | "Many," "fast," "better" | Specific: "847," "14 days," "3x" |
| **Generic CTAs** | "Submit," "Learn More" | First-person, outcome: "Get My Guide" |
| **Uniform sentences** | All same length/structure | Vary. Short. Medium. Long when needed. |
| **Clever over clear** | Puns, wordplay | Clear always wins |
| **College-level reading** | 5.3% conversion | 5th-7th grade: 11.1% conversion |

## Output

When optimizing a landing page, produce **rewritten copy by section**:

1. **Hero** — Headline, subheadline, CTA
2. **Value proposition** — Benefits bullets
3. **Social proof** — Testimonial rewrites or recommendations
4. **Objection handling** — FAQ or how-it-works copy
5. **Final CTA** — Closing section with urgency

For each section, apply the principles. Don't produce analysis documents. Don't explain rationale at length. Produce copy the user can implement.

If specific changes need explanation, add a brief note after the copy.

- [ ] No AI tells (delve, robust, em dashes, uniform sentences)
