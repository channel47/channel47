# Variation Dimensions

Exhaustive list of testable elements in ad creatives. Use these to plan systematic A/B tests that isolate specific variables.

---

## Tier 1: Highest Impact Variables

Test these first—they typically drive the largest performance swings.

### Headline/Text Content

**What to vary:**
- Value proposition angle (benefit vs. feature)
- Emotional tone (fear vs. aspiration)
- Specificity level ("Save time" vs. "Save 4 hours/week")
- Question vs. statement
- First person vs. second person ("I did X" vs. "You can X")
- Length (short punch vs. complete thought)

**Example test pairs:**
- "Lose Weight Fast" vs. "Lose 10 lbs in 30 Days"
- "Stop Wasting Time" vs. "Get Back 2 Hours Every Day"
- "The Best CRM" vs. "Why 10,000 Teams Switched"

### Primary Image Subject

**What to vary:**
- Product alone vs. product in use
- Person vs. no person
- Close-up vs. context shot
- Single item vs. collection/bundle
- Real photo vs. illustration
- Before/after vs. after only

**Example test pairs:**
- Supplement bottle vs. person holding bottle
- Software screenshot vs. happy user at computer
- Product on white vs. product in lifestyle setting

### Call-to-Action

**What to vary:**
- CTA text ("Get Started" vs. "Claim Your Spot")
- CTA color (contrast level)
- CTA size (subtle vs. dominant)
- CTA position (bottom vs. overlay)
- Single CTA vs. multiple CTAs
- Button vs. link style

**Example test pairs:**
- "Learn More" vs. "See How It Works"
- Red button vs. green button
- Small corner CTA vs. large center CTA

---

## Tier 2: Significant Impact Variables

Test these after Tier 1 winners are established.

### Color Scheme

**What to vary:**
- Background color (warm vs. cool)
- Accent color selection
- Color saturation (vibrant vs. muted)
- Color contrast levels
- Brand color dominance
- Monochrome vs. multi-color

**Example test pairs:**
- White background vs. brand blue background
- High-saturation product colors vs. muted lifestyle palette
- Single accent color vs. complementary accent pair

### Human Elements

**What to vary:**
- Presence/absence of people
- Demographics (age, gender, ethnicity)
- Expression (happy, serious, surprised)
- Gaze direction (at camera vs. at product)
- Professional vs. casual appearance
- Individual vs. group

**Example test pairs:**
- Solo user vs. team using product
- Young demographic vs. target demographic match
- Direct eye contact vs. looking at product

### Layout/Composition

**What to vary:**
- Text position (top, center, bottom)
- Image-to-text ratio
- Symmetrical vs. asymmetrical balance
- Grid vs. organic layout
- Negative space amount
- Element density

**Example test pairs:**
- Text overlay on image vs. text beside image
- Minimal layout vs. information-dense layout
- Center-focused vs. rule-of-thirds composition

---

## Tier 3: Refinement Variables

Test these to optimize already-performing creatives.

### Typography

**What to vary:**
- Font style (serif vs. sans-serif)
- Font weight (bold vs. regular)
- Text size hierarchy
- Text color/contrast
- Text effects (shadow, outline)
- Case (all caps vs. sentence case)

**Example test pairs:**
- Bold sans-serif vs. elegant serif
- ALL CAPS headline vs. Title Case
- High-contrast text vs. subtle overlay text

### Social Proof Elements

**What to vary:**
- Type (testimonial, rating, user count, logos)
- Prominence (hero vs. supporting)
- Format (quote vs. statistic vs. badge)
- Specificity (named person vs. anonymous)
- Quantity shown (single proof vs. multiple)

**Example test pairs:**
- Star rating badge vs. written testimonial
- "10,000+ users" vs. specific customer logos
- Single strong testimonial vs. three short ones

### Urgency/Scarcity Indicators

**What to vary:**
- Presence/absence of urgency
- Urgency type (time vs. quantity)
- Visual treatment (badge, banner, timer)
- Placement (corner vs. prominent)
- Language intensity

**Example test pairs:**
- No urgency vs. "Limited time offer"
- Countdown timer vs. "Only 3 left"
- Subtle corner badge vs. banner across image

---

## Tier 4: Micro-Optimization Variables

Test these for marginal gains after major elements are optimized.

### Image Quality/Style

**What to vary:**
- Photo vs. illustration
- Professional vs. UGC aesthetic
- Filter/color grading
- Lighting style (bright vs. moody)
- Background blur amount
- Border/frame presence

### Logo/Branding

**What to vary:**
- Logo size
- Logo position
- Logo color variation
- Tagline presence
- Brand mark vs. full logo

### Supporting Elements

**What to vary:**
- Icons presence/style
- Decorative elements
- Dividers/separators
- Badges and seals
- Texture/pattern backgrounds

## Platform-Specific Considerations

### Facebook/Instagram Feed

**Priority variables:**
- Pattern interrupt (critical for feed)
- Mobile readability (text size)
- Square vs. 4:5 aspect ratio
- First-frame hook (video thumbnails)

**Platform constraints:**
- Text-to-image ratio (historically <20%)
- Safe zones for UI overlay
- Autoplay considerations

### Google Display Network

**Priority variables:**
- Brand clarity (fast recognition)
- CTA prominence
- Size adaptation (responsive creatives)

**Platform constraints:**
- Multiple size requirements
- File size limits
- Animation restrictions

### LinkedIn

**Priority variables:**
- Professional aesthetic
- Authority signals
- B2B value proposition clarity

**Platform constraints:**
- More text-tolerant
- Professional context expectations

### TikTok/Reels

**Priority variables:**
- Native aesthetic (not "ad-like")
- Motion/energy
- Vertical optimization
- First 0.5-second hook

**Platform constraints:**
- Must feel organic
- Sound-on considerations
- Vertical-only format

## Test Design Principles

### Isolation Rule

Change ONLY 1-2 variables per variant. Otherwise, you can't attribute results.

**Good test structure:**
```
Control: Blue background, "Get Started" CTA
Variant 1: Green background, "Get Started" CTA (testing: background color)
Variant 2: Blue background, "Start Free Trial" CTA (testing: CTA copy)
Variant 3: Green background, "Start Free Trial" CTA (testing: interaction)
```

**Bad test structure:**
```
Control: Blue background, product image, "Get Started" CTA
Variant 1: Green background, lifestyle image, "Try It Free" CTA
(Three variables changed—impossible to attribute results)
```

### Statistical Validity

Minimum requirements before declaring a winner:
- 1,000+ impressions per variant
- 95% confidence level
- Run for at least 7 days (day-of-week effects)
- No major external factors during test

### Learning Hierarchy

Build knowledge systematically:

1. **Test big swings first** - Completely different approaches
2. **Optimize the winner** - Refine best performer
3. **Test interactions** - Combine winning elements
4. **Scale and repeat** - Apply learnings to new creatives

## Variation Matrix Template

Plan tests systematically:

| Variable | Control | Variant A | Variant B | Variant C |
|----------|---------|-----------|-----------|-----------|
| Headline | "Save Time" | "Save 4 Hours/Week" | - | - |
| Background | White | - | Blue | - |
| CTA | "Learn More" | - | - | "Start Free" |

This ensures each variant isolates a single change for clean attribution.
