# Persona Examples Across DTC Verticals

These are calibration examples showing the depth and specificity expected from data-driven personas. Each was built from real research data.

---

## Example 1: Pet Training Device

### Persona: "The Desperate Dog Mom"

**The Snapshot**
- Age Range: 30-45
- Gender Skew: ~70% female
- Life Situation: Works from home or has flexible schedule. Dog is a rescue or adopted during COVID. Lives in apartment or townhouse with shared walls/close neighbors.
- Income Signal: Moderate — willing to spend $30-80 but wants proof before spending more. Has already spent money on training that didn't work.
- Tech Comfort: Comfortable shopping online, reads reviews before buying.

**The Internal Monologue**
> "I love my dog so much but I'm at my breaking point. He barks at EVERYTHING — the doorbell, other dogs, the mail carrier, literally a leaf blowing by. I've watched every YouTube video, tried the trainer treats, even hired a trainer for $200 and he was good for like a week. My neighbor left a note on my door last week and I was honestly mortified. I just need something that actually works. I don't care what it costs at this point, I just can't keep living like this."

**The Trigger Event**
- Primary: Neighbor complaint (note on door, awkward hallway encounter, formal complaint to landlord)
- Secondary: Embarrassment during walks, being unable to have guests over, sleep disruption

**Pain Points (ranked)**
1. Social embarrassment — "my neighbors must hate me"
2. Feeling like a failure as a pet owner — "I'm clearly doing something wrong"
3. Disruption to daily life — can't work from home in peace, can't have video calls

**Desired Outcome**
- Stated: "I want my dog to stop barking"
- Deeper: "I want to enjoy having a dog again and not feel constant anxiety about the noise"

**Objections & Skepticism**
1. "These things never work — I've tried everything" (solution fatigue)
2. "Is it going to hurt my dog?" (ethical concern)
3. "It probably only works for some dogs, not mine" (special-case thinking)

**What They've Already Tried**
- Positive reinforcement training — "worked temporarily, then he went back to barking"
- Professional trainer — "too expensive and the effects didn't last"
- Bark collar — "felt cruel, didn't want to use it"
- Ignoring it — "just got worse"

**Language Fingerprint**
- "I've tried everything"
- "at my breaking point"
- "my neighbor complained"
- "I love my dog but..."
- "does this actually work?"
- "game changer" (when something finally works)

**Ad Responsiveness Signals**
- Hook style: Social proof + empathy ("If your dog's barking is driving your neighbors crazy...")
- Proof needed: Video demonstrations showing before/after, real customer testimonials
- CTA style: Risk-reversal ("Try it for 30 days, full refund if it doesn't work")

## Example 2: Toilet Cleaning Product

### Persona: "The Grossed-Out Husband"

**The Snapshot**
- Age Range: 28-40
- Gender Skew: ~55% male (surprising — this persona is often assumed to be female)
- Life Situation: Lives with partner/spouse. Recently became responsible for bathroom cleaning (new living arrangement, partner is pregnant, or just started caring about it).
- Income Signal: Not price-sensitive for cleaning products — cares more about effectiveness. Will pay $15-25 for something that works.
- Tech Comfort: Shops online by default, influenced by social media product discovery.

**The Internal Monologue**
> "I didn't realize how disgusting toilet rings were until I actually started cleaning the bathroom. The regular toilet bowl cleaner doesn't do anything. I've been scrubbing for 20 minutes and it still looks nasty. There has to be something that just... handles this without me having to get in there with a brush every week. My buddy showed me something on TikTok that looked like it worked but I can't remember what it was called."

**The Trigger Event**
- Primary: Visible ring/stain that won't come off with regular cleaning
- Secondary: Having guests over and realizing the bathroom looks bad, partner making a comment

**Pain Points (ranked)**
1. Regular products don't actually clean tough stains
2. Scrubbing is gross and time-consuming
3. Embarrassment when guests use the bathroom

**Desired Outcome**
- Stated: "I want a clean toilet without effort"
- Deeper: "I want to feel like I have my living situation together without spending my life cleaning"

**Objections & Skepticism**
1. "Every cleaning product claims to be the best" (advertising fatigue)
2. "Is this actually different from Clorox?" (commodity perception)
3. "Those 'before and after' videos are probably fake" (social media skepticism)

**What They've Already Tried**
- Clorox toilet bowl cleaner — "doesn't get rid of the hard water ring"
- Pumice stone — "works but takes forever and I hate doing it"
- Bleach — "makes it white for a day then the ring comes back"

**Language Fingerprint**
- "nothing works on hard water stains"
- "I don't want to scrub"
- "is this legit or another gimmick?"
- "just want it to do the work for me"
- "drop it in and forget about it"

**Ad Responsiveness Signals**
- Hook style: Demonstration-led ("Watch what happens when I drop this in a toilet that hasn't been cleaned in 3 months")
- Proof needed: Video demonstration is essential — show the product working in real-time
- CTA style: Low-friction curiosity ("See it in action — link in bio")

## Using These Examples for Calibration

When building personas from research data, calibrate against these examples:

1. **Specificity level** — Notice how each persona has specific life details, not generic demographics
2. **Internal monologue** — Written in casual, conversational first-person using language patterns from real data
3. **Pain hierarchy** — Pain points are ranked, not just listed
4. **Stated vs. deeper desire** — Always separate what they say they want from what they really want
5. **Objections are specific** — Not "price concern" but "every cleaning product claims to be the best"
6. **Language fingerprint** — Exact phrases, not paraphrased summaries
7. **Ad responsiveness** — Directly actionable for creative teams
