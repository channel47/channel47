# Analysis Rubric

Scoring criteria for consistent, rigorous competitor analysis and gap identification. Reference this when evaluating competitors and validating opportunities to ensure you're not skipping steps or softening findings.

---

## Competitor Analysis Scoring

### Extraction Completeness

For each Direct competitor, verify you've captured all eight elements:

| Element | Captured? | Source Verified? |
|---------|-----------|-----------------|
| Positioning statement | Extracted from actual homepage, not assumed | Fetched and read their page |
| Primary audience | Inferred from pricing, case studies, language | Multiple signals, not just "About Us" |
| Key differentiator | What they emphasize most, not what you think | Feature page, comparison pages checked |
| Proof points | Specific numbers, logos, case studies | On their actual site |
| Pricing signal | Actual pricing or tier structure | Pricing page visited |
| Messaging tone | Overall voice characterization | Read multiple pages, not just homepage |
| Weaknesses | Sourced from reviews or community | External evidence, not your speculation |
| Content strategy | What topics they invest in | Blog, social, YouTube checked |

**Minimum standard:** At least 6/8 elements for Direct competitors. All 8 if web search is available and the competitor has a public web presence.

### Threat Assessment

Rate each competitor's relevance to the user's situation:

| Factor | Weight | How to Assess |
|--------|--------|--------------|
| **Audience overlap** | High | Do they target the same specific people, or just the same broad category? |
| **Solution similarity** | Medium | Could a customer reasonably see them as interchangeable? |
| **Awareness among target audience** | High | Would the user's target customers have heard of them? |
| **Momentum** | Low | Are they growing, stable, or declining? (Check for recent layoffs, pivots, or funding) |

Don't treat all competitors equally. A direct competitor with high audience overlap matters 10x more than an adjacent competitor with broad reach.

## Gap Identification Rigor

### Evidence Standards

Every gap must be supported by at least one of these evidence types:

| Evidence Type | Strength | Example |
|--------------|----------|---------|
| **Customer review quotes** | Strong | G2 review: "great for teams but overkill for solo users" |
| **Community discussion** | Strong | Reddit thread with 50+ upvotes asking for exactly this |
| **Search query data** | Strong | Autocomplete or People Also Ask showing unmet demand |
| **Competitor absence** | Moderate | No competitor has a landing page, feature, or content for this |
| **Logical inference** | Weak | "It stands to reason that..." (use only when paired with stronger evidence) |

**Minimum standard:** Every gap in the final brief must have at least one Strong evidence source. Gaps supported only by inference should be flagged as hypotheses, not findings.

### Gap Validation Checklist

Apply all four tests to every gap before including it in the recommendation:

| Test | Question | Failing Answer |
|------|----------|---------------|
| **Demand** | Do people search for this, complain about its absence, or ask for it? | "It seems like people would want this" (no evidence) |
| **Credibility** | Can the user's product/service actually deliver on this? | "They could probably build this" (feature doesn't exist) |
| **Defensibility** | Would a well-funded competitor close this gap in 90 days? | "It's just a messaging change" (easily copied) |
| **Materiality** | Is this a buying-decision-level concern? | "It would be a nice bonus" (not a switching trigger) |

**Scoring:**
- 4/4 pass → Include in recommendation with confidence
- 3/4 pass → Include with the caveat noted
- 2/4 pass → Mention as a secondary observation, not a primary gap
- 1/4 or 0/4 → Drop it. Including weak gaps dilutes the strong ones.

## Recommendation Quality

### Positioning Statement Test

The final recommended position should pass the **"Unlike" test:**

> "[Product] is the [frame] for [audience] who [need], **unlike** [alternative] which [limitation]."

If you can't fill in the "unlike" clause with something specific and true, the positioning isn't differentiated enough.

### Specificity Checks

| Element | Vague (Fail) | Specific (Pass) |
|---------|-------------|-----------------|
| **Audience** | "Content creators" | "Freelance copywriters managing 5+ client accounts" |
| **Value** | "Better writing" | "Match any client's voice from the first draft" |
| **Frame** | "AI writing tool" | "Client voice management tool" |
| **Proof** | "Trusted by many" | "Sarah manages 12 client voices and cut onboarding time by 60%" |
| **Next step** | "Consider repositioning" | "Rewrite homepage with new tagline, run $500 Reddit ad test" |

### Sacrifice Test

If the recommendation doesn't explicitly name what the user is giving up, it's not positioning — it's wishful thinking.

Check for:
- **Audience sacrifice:** Who are you deliberately NOT targeting?
- **Territory sacrifice:** What claims are you choosing NOT to make?
- **Channel sacrifice:** Where are you choosing NOT to compete?

If the recommendation sounds like it appeals to everyone, it appeals to no one. Push back on yourself until the sacrifice is clear.

## Output Quality Scoring

Before delivering the final brief, self-score on these criteria:

| Criterion | Score (1-5) | What 5 Looks Like |
|-----------|-------------|-------------------|
| **Novel insight** | | Contains at least one finding the user didn't already have |
| **Evidence-backed** | | Every major claim linked to a specific source |
| **Actionable** | | User can take the first step today without more research |
| **Specific** | | No "differentiate on quality," no "target SMBs" |
| **Opinionated** | | One clear recommendation, not a menu of options |
| **Honest about tradeoffs** | | Sacrifices named, risks acknowledged |

**Minimum acceptable score: 4+ on at least 4/6 criteria.** If you're scoring 3s, the analysis needs another pass.

## Common Failure Modes

### "Everything Is a Gap"

If you're finding 6+ gaps, you're not being selective enough. Most markets have 2-3 real gaps and a lot of mirages. Apply the validation checklist ruthlessly and cut the weak ones.

### "The Gap Is Just 'Be Better'"

"Competitors have bad UX" or "Nobody explains their product clearly" — these aren't positioning gaps. They're execution problems. A positioning gap is structural: a specific audience, job, awareness level, or frame that is unoccupied.

### "The Recommendation Is a Rebrand"

Changing the tagline isn't repositioning. If the recommendation is just "say different words," the analysis hasn't gone deep enough. Real positioning changes what features you emphasize, who you build for, where you market, and what you choose not to do.

### "No Real Competitors Found"

If you can't find direct competitors, either:
1. The category doesn't exist yet (which is a finding — apply category design framework)
2. You're searching for the wrong thing (broaden to the *job*, not the product type)
3. The incumbent is "do nothing" (make that the primary competitor)

Never tell the user "you have no competitors." Everyone competes with something, even if it's inertia.
