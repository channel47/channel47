# Conversion Copy. ch47.

Operational patterns for landing pages, emails, and voice-native persuasion. The philosophy lives in SKILL.md (Persuasion Philosophy section). This file is the how.

## Landing Page Copy

A ch47 landing page is a narrative with a conversion point. Not a funnel with sections. The reader should feel like they learned something or had their thinking shifted, and the CTA arrives as a natural conclusion.

### Density Map as Persuasion Architecture

The density map (from the frontend-design skill) doubles as a persuasion structure. Each density zone does persuasion work.

```
LOW  (hero)       Thesis. One clear claim. Space creates authority.
HIGH (proof)      Credibility. Stats, specifics, evidence. Dense and quiet.
MEDIUM (narrative)  Story. Why this exists. The builder's perspective.
RUPTURE           Frame shift. Changes how the reader sees the problem.
MEDIUM (ask)      The offer. What they get. Plain and direct.
LOW  (close)      CTA. Void around it. Confidence through restraint.
```

The rupture is where persuasion actually happens. Everything before it builds the frame. The rupture breaks it. The reader sees the problem differently. The CTA after a rupture feels like their idea, not yours.

### Landing Page Beat Pattern

Six beats. Not all mandatory, but this is the default arc.

**1. Claim (hero).** A thesis about the problem or the approach. Not a benefit statement. Not "Save 10 hours a week." More like "Stop clicking. Start supervising." The claim should make the right reader nod and the wrong reader leave. That's filtering, not losing people.

**2. Credentials (proof strip).** Quiet evidence. Numbers, not adjectives. "25+ accounts managed" not "trusted by industry leaders." Stats strip format. Low opacity, small type. Credibility whispered, not declared.

**3. Origin (narrative).** Why this exists. First person. The specific moment it clicked. "I manage 25+ accounts. The job isn't strategy most days. It's the same checklist on repeat." Origin creates empathy and authority simultaneously. The reader thinks "this person actually does this work."

**4. Mechanism (features).** What it does and how. Not benefits. Mechanism. "The agent runs a loop with guardrails so you approve changes instead of living in the UI." Mechanism-first writing respects the reader's intelligence. Let them derive the benefits.

**5. Rupture (frame shift).** The moment that changes the conversation. Surface flip (dark to light). Density shift. And a statement that reframes. "This isn't vaporware." "The plugin already runs in production on my accounts." The rupture earns the ask by establishing stakes. Something real is happening. Not a pitch deck. A thing that exists.

**6. Ask (CTA).** Plain. Direct. Describes what happens. "Get build notes." "Try it." "Subscribe." One CTA per page gets the Signal-fill button. Everything else is a text link. The void around the CTA is the emphasis. Don't add more. Add space.

### Three Hero Formulas

**The Reframe.** Takes a familiar concept and rotates it. The reader's assumption gets corrected in the headline itself.
- "Stop clicking. Start supervising."
- "Your ad account doesn't need more features. It needs fewer mistakes."
- "The best media buyer is the one who isn't clicking buttons."

**The Artifact.** A specific, concrete detail that implies the whole story.
- "One script caught a negative keyword gap burning ~$3K/month."
- "$3K/month in wasted spend. One script. Two accounts."
- "52 issues shipped. Zero hype."

**The Invitation.** Brings the reader into an ongoing thing. Not a launch. A frequency.
- "I build with AI and share what works."
- "Building an agent in public. Notes inside."
- "The build is open. Join the frequency."

### CTA Patterns

Button text describes what happens, not what to feel.

| Kill | Use |
|---|---|
| "Start Your Journey" | "Get build notes" |
| "Unlock Your Potential" | "Try it" |
| "Join the Revolution" | "Subscribe" |
| "Claim Your Free Guide" | "Download" |
| "Yes, I Want In!" | "Get started" |

One Signal-fill CTA button per page. Additional CTAs are text links with Signal underline or outline buttons. The restraint creates hierarchy. If everything is a hot button, nothing is.

### Objection Handling

No FAQ sections. Objections are handled through narrative specificity.

The reader's objection ("Is this real?") gets answered by the origin story's specificity. The reader's objection ("Will this work for me?") gets answered by the mechanism section's clarity. The reader's objection ("Why should I trust this person?") gets answered by the proof strip and the first-person provenance.

If an objection can't be handled through the narrative, address it directly in the rupture section. One sentence. "This isn't vaporware." "The plugin runs on my accounts." Direct statement, not defensive explanation.

## Email Sequence Copy

### Email Voice Calibration

Same voice, compressed container. The email is the newsletter's leaner sibling.

- **Length.** 150-300 words. One screen on mobile. The reader decides in 3 seconds whether to keep reading.
- **One idea.** Every email has exactly one idea. Not three quick hits. One thing, explored enough to be useful.
- **First person "I".** Emails are personal. "I shipped something this week" not "We're excited to announce."
- **PS lines.** Welcome. The PS is where the secondary link or aside lives. Feels human. "PS: The build log for this one is messy. Fair warning."
- **Subject lines.** Use the same opening moves as editorial content. Cold Start, Artifact, Observation. Never clickbait, never urgency language, never ALL CAPS.

### Welcome Sequence

Four emails. Each earns the next open.

**Email 1: The Delivery.** Arrives immediately. Delivers what was promised (the lead magnet, the resource, the first build note). Short. 100-150 words. "Here's what you signed up for. [Link]. One more thing:" followed by a single sentence about what's coming. No life story. No "I'm so glad you're here." Deliver, set expectation, done.

**Email 2: The Proof Moment.** Day 2-3. One specific, useful thing. A working automation. A prompt template. A technique. The reader should be able to act on this today. This email proves the subscription is worth keeping. "Last week I caught a $3K/month negative keyword gap using this script. Here's the script."

**Email 3: The Worldview.** Day 5-7. The contrarian take or the origin story. Why ch47 exists. What you believe about the space. This is the identity email. The reader either resonates or unsubscribes. Both outcomes are good. "Most AI content is noise. I'm trying to build the signal."

**Email 4: The Invitation.** Day 10-14. The soft ask. Follow on X. Join the community. Check out the tools. Not a hard sell. An invitation to go deeper. "If any of this is useful, here's where more of it lives."

### Nurture Pattern

After the welcome sequence, ongoing emails follow one rule: every email IS value. The promotion lives inside useful content. Never a separate "and now the pitch" section.

**Structure.** Useful content (80%) with a natural mention of the relevant thing (20%). The mention is contextual, not bolted on. "I used [tool] to build this automation" is natural. "Speaking of tools, check out [tool]" is bolted on.

**Frequency.** When there's something worth sharing. Not on a schedule for the sake of a schedule. The reader should think "oh, new build note" not "oh, Tuesday again."

### Launch / Announcement

Build-up happens through regular content, not a dedicated "launch sequence."

**The pattern:**
1. Regular content mentions the thing being built, naturally, over weeks.
2. Ship email leads with the thing. "It's live. Here's what it does. Here's the link." 200 words max. No buildup in the email itself because the buildup happened over weeks of content.
3. Follow-up is a build log about the launch itself. What worked, what didn't, what's next. This IS content. The launch follow-up is a build log, not a "thank you for your support" email.

**What never happens:** Cart-close emails. Last-chance emails. Countdown timers in email. "Did you forget?" re-engagement. Urgency language of any kind. If the thing is good and the audience is right, one clear announcement is enough. If it's not enough, the problem isn't the email.

## Voice-Native Persuasion Mechanics

Standard persuasion principles mapped to ch47 execution. The principles work. The execution is what makes them ch47.

| Principle | Standard execution | ch47 execution |
|---|---|---|
| **Social proof** | "Join 10,000+ happy customers" badge | Stats strip with specific real numbers. "25+ accounts managed. $3K waste caught/month." No adjectives. |
| **Authority** | Logos, certifications, "as seen in" | First-person provenance. "I manage these accounts. I built this tool. Here's the code." Authority earned through specificity, not declared through badges. |
| **Reciprocity** | Free ebook gated behind email | Structural generosity. The build notes, the open-source code, the process documentation. Give the valuable thing. Then mention the email list exists. |
| **Scarcity** | "Only 3 spots left!" countdown | Honest status. "Ships when it's ready." "Open source when it's done." Real constraints stated plainly. Never manufactured. |
| **Liking** | Polished founder story, professional headshot | Dry humor, admitted failures, showing the messy build. "It deleted staging twice." Liking through honesty, not polish. |
| **Commitment** | "Start your free trial" → upgrade funnel | Progressive value. Build notes → useful scripts → deeper engagement. Each step delivers before asking for the next. |
| **Unity** | "Join our community of innovators" | Tribe recognition. "For builders, tinkerers, and skeptical optimists." The reader recognizes themselves. No invitation needed. |

### The Conversion Test

Before shipping any conversion-oriented content, run it through these filters:

1. **The whisper test.** Read the CTA out loud in a normal speaking voice. If it sounds like shouting, rewrite. "Get build notes" passes. "UNLOCK YOUR FREE ACCESS NOW" fails.
2. **The value ratio.** Count the words of value delivered vs. words of ask. The ratio should be at least 5:1. If the page is 80% pitch and 20% content, invert it.
3. **The stated intent test.** Is the conversion goal obvious from the beginning? The reader should know within the first screen that this page has something they can sign up for or buy. No bait-and-switch. No "surprise" CTAs.
4. **The remove test.** Take out every persuasion element (stats, proof, social, urgency). Does the content still stand on its own as useful or interesting? If yes, the persuasion elements are additive. If no, the content is a vehicle for selling, not a piece of value with a conversion point.
